# The Coleman operator on whole cross-sections (attempt, 2026-11-11)

Context: `COLEMAN_PLAN.md` gives, for the representative agent, a recursive equilibrium on `(z, K)`
at every capital share by Tarski on the Coleman operator. This file records the attempt to carry
the operator to heterogeneous households.

## The operator

Unknown: a saving rule on the whole cross-section, `σ(a, e, h)` at every node `h` of the tree
(equivalently a rule on `(z, μ, a, e)` if it is recursive). Two legs:

* **household leg**: given a price path, the Euler root at every `(a, e, h)` against next period's
  consumption under the rule;
* **aggregation leg**: the price path is generated by the rule: `K_h` = mean assets of the
  cross-section the rule generates from `μ₀`.

For the representative agent the cross-section is a point, own saving is aggregate saving, and the
aggregation leg disappears into the Euler root (`B(s)` evaluates the return at `s`).

## Proved (`CrossSectionEuler.lean`, standard axioms)

* `IsEulerRule`, `rulePlan`, **`policy_eq_of_eulerRule`**: verification on the cross-section — a
  rule satisfying the Euler conditions with corners at every `(a, e, h)`, with bounded marginal
  utility, is the household's optimal policy at every state (any `KSModel`, no borrowing).
* `ruleDist`, `IsCrossSectionEquilibrium` (the fixed-point property of the two-leg operator),
  **`IsCrossSectionEquilibrium.isSequentialEquilibrium`**.
* **`isCrossSectionEquilibrium_of_sequential`**: the converse, from Euler necessity
  (`euler_le`/`euler_ge`) when consumption is bounded away from the floor.
* **`crra_isSequentialEquilibrium_iff`**, **`crra_exists_crossSectionEquilibrium`**: for CRRA the
  fixed points of the cross-section operator are exactly the sequential equilibria, so one exists
  from every initial distribution at every capital share — existence by Schauder–Tychonoff.

## Not obtained

An order argument, and with it a recursive equilibrium on `(z, μ)`.

* The household leg is monotone in next period's policy (as for the representative agent).
* The aggregation leg is not: a change in the rule moves `K`, and a price change redistributes
  cash, `∂cash/∂K = R'(K) a + w'(K) e`, with `∫ = 0` over the cross-section
  (`CobbDouglas.integral_redistribution_eq_zero`). Households long in assets and households long in
  labour move in opposite directions; pointwise orders on rules are not preserved (the earlier
  negative result R1), and heterogeneous step (b) of `EXISTENCE_EXTENSIONS_PLAN.md` fails pointwise
  for the same reason.
* Without order, the inner problem (find today's savings consistent with tomorrow's cross-section)
  needs continuity of the continuation in `μ'`, which a Tarski fixed point does not provide.

## Routes not yet tried

1. **Mixed monotonicity in an aggregate order.** Order rules by the capital paths they generate
   rather than pointwise; the bracket machinery of `AntitoneEquilibrium.lean` then needs the
   aggregate of the redistributed cash to move the right way (true: it is `∂(RK + wL)/∂K ≥ 0`) and a
   bound on the dispersion term. This is step (b) in aggregate form.
2. **No-redistribution economies.** Technologies/tax schemes for which `R'(K) a + w'(K) e` has one
   sign on the support (e.g. assets proportional to efficiency), where the pointwise argument goes
   through.
3. **Recursive selection** from the set of cross-section Euler equilibria (measurable selection,
   item 5 of `EXISTENCE_EXTENSIONS_PLAN.md`) rather than an order argument.

## Route 1 tried 2026-11-11 — stops at depth one (`CrossSectionAggregateOrder.lean`)

Question: does the expectations map reverse the aggregate order (`K ≤ K'` expected ⇒ realised
capital lower at every node) for heterogeneous households, as from point masses?

* **Depth one: yes** (`expMap_child_le`, any initial cross-section; CRRA `γ ≤ 1`, slack
  hypotheses of step (a)). At the root both expectations face the same cross-section and prices.
* **Deeper: only under a dispersion bound** (`CobbDouglas.cash_le_of_ratio`,
  `grossReturn_mul_ratio_le`). With realised capital `A' ≤ A`, household cash is ordered if
  `a' ≤ (A'/A)^{1−α} a`: each household's assets must fall, in logs, by at least `(1 − α)` times the
  aggregate fall. Exact for the representative agent and for proportionally scaled
  cross-sections; in general it is an elasticity condition on household saving, not a consequence
  of the primitives (the same kind of condition as B3).
* **Mechanism for failure.** A household that does not cut its assets (at the cap, or
  insensitive to expectations) gains cash from the higher return and saves more next period; if
  the rest of the cross-section is at the zero-saving corner, aggregate saving rises when capital
  falls. Not formalised as a counterexample economy (it needs an explicit household solution, as
  for the corner witness).
* Dominance instead of coupling (`AssetDominates`) is more demanding still: it compares saving at
  the *same* assets, losing the slack `a' < a`.

Conclusion: the aggregate order does not rescue the order argument. Remaining: route 2
(no-redistribution economies — the equality case of the dispersion bound) and route 3 (selection).

## Route 2 done 2026-11-11 — the proportional economy (`ProportionalEquilibrium.lean`)

The equality case of the dispersion bound: **permanent** efficiency types
(`idioProb z z' e e' = [e = e']`) holding assets **proportional to efficiency**, type `e` the share
`θ_e = efficiency e / L` of aggregate capital (`L` = mean efficiency).

* `ColemanSufficiency.lean` generalised: `PlanEulerOn`, `value_le_of_eulerOn`,
  **`policy_eq_of_eulerOn`** — Euler sufficiency needs the conditions only on histories of positive
  probability (the old statements are corollaries).
* `aggData` (aggregate economy as Coleman data, labour `L`, capital cap `cap` with
  `θ_e cap ≤ ā`); `propRule` (share of the Coleman saving on the path, zero off it),
  `onPath_of_pos`; `ruleRhs_onPath` (expected marginal value = `θ_e^{-γ} ×` Coleman continuation,
  CRRA homogeneity); `propPlan_euler`; **`policy_eq_prop`**: type `e` at assets `θ_e k` saves
  `θ_e s*(z, k)`.
* `propDist` (masses `p e` at `(θ_e K, e)`), `nodeDist_propDist` (stays proportional),
  **`isSequentialEquilibrium_prop`**: from every proportional cross-section the aggregate Coleman
  path is a sequential equilibrium, capital following `K' = s*(z, K)`; every capital share.
  Standard axioms.
* Hypotheses: `β R̄ ≤ 1`, `L` in the labour box, and the capital cap slack at the fixed point
  (`hslack`; `Data.saving_lt_cap_of`: enough that `y(z, k) − m < cap`). The cap must be slack
  because types reach their own asset cap at different aggregate capitals.
* Scope: wealth shares are constant, so heterogeneity is in levels only (Gorman aggregation); the
  cross-section is a function of `K`. Not done: the `AggStateRecursiveEquilibrium` packaging
  (as `colemanRecursive`), with `dist z K` the proportional cross-section.

Remaining: route 3 (selection). Idiosyncratic risk or non-proportional wealth is back to the
dispersion problem.

## Route 2 packaged 2026-11-11 (`ProportionalRecursive.lean`)

**`propRecursive`**: an `AggStateRecursiveEquilibrium` on `(z, K)`, `K ∈ [0, cap]`, for the

## Route 3 tried 2026-11-11 — selection does not give the minimal state (`SelectionObstruction.lean`)

A recursive equilibrium on `(z, μ)` is a **consistent** selection of sequential equilibria
(`ConsistentSelection.isRecursive`, already in the repo). The question is whether a selection
theorem can supply consistency. Analysis (on paper), then what was formalised.

* **Measurable selection (Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski type).** The equilibrium correspondence
  `(z, μ) ↦ Eq(z, μ)` is nonempty, compact-valued with closed graph, so a Borel selection exists in
  principle. It is not consistent: consistency is a functional equation on the selection,
  `shift (σ x) [z'] = σ (child of x)`, which a selection theorem does not see.
* **Markov / semiflow selection (Krylov; Stroock–Varadhan ch. 12).** These do produce consistent
  selections, by lexicographic optimisation, but need *concatenation*: a solution followed by a
  solution from the point reached is a solution. Here it fails. Households are forward-looking, so
  replacing the continuation below a node changes saving at its ancestors and hence the
  cross-section at the node. Concatenation holds on a state that carries the continuation (the
  value-function state, `ValueState*.lean`), which is why those results exist and the minimal-state
  one does not.
* **Sequence-state version** (item 5 of `EXISTENCE_EXTENSIONS_PLAN.md`). A measurable
  sequence-state equilibrium is an equivariant measurable selection from the pullback sets
  `S(ω) = ⋂_T Φ_{ω₀} ∘ ⋯ ∘ Φ_{ω_{T−1}}(𝒢)`. Equivariant selections correspond to a choice per
  tail-equivalence class of histories, and tail equivalence has no measurable transversal; the
  measurable object that does exist is an invariant random measure (the randomised equilibrium
  already proved). Tail functionals (`limsup`) are equivariant but not continuous, so their
  maximisers need not exist.
* **Formalised**: `ConsistentSelection.shiftPath_eq_of_merge` (selected equilibria that reach the
  same shock and cross-section continue identically), `shiftPath_eq_of_node_eq` (a selected
  equilibrium is Markov on its own tree), and **`isEmpty_consistentSelection_of_conflict`**: two
  states with unique sequential equilibria reaching the same state with different continuations
  rule out every consistent selection. This is the shape of a Kubler–Schmedders-type
  counterexample; not constructed here (it needs an explicit economy with multiple equilibria).
* **Known cure, not available here**: Brumm–Kryczka–Kubler / He–Sun obtain the minimal state with
  an atomless component in the exogenous shock, which convexifies expected continuations. With
  finitely many aggregate shocks there is nothing to convexify. (Literature cited from memory.)

Conclusion: all three routes are closed. Minimal-state recursive equilibria are proved where
there is structure — uniqueness (small shares), monotone saving (complementarities), the
representative agent and the proportional economy (Coleman) — and on enlarged states otherwise.

## Literature checked 2026-11-11

* **Brumm–Kryczka–Kubler (Econometrica 2017), He–Sun (JET 2017).** The convexification needs a
  purely transitory atomless component in the shock (a coarser transition kernel). A plain AR(1)
  does not qualify, and both results are for finitely many agents with finite-dimensional
  convexity. Cao (JET 2020) calls the minimal-state Krusell–Smith equilibrium "elusive".
* **Pröhl (working paper, 2018 version read in full).** Claims existence and uniqueness for
  Krusell–Smith by maximal monotonicity of a stacked Euler operator in `L²`. The proof does not
  hold up as written: the admissible set is not a Hilbert space; the monotonicity lemma is one
  sentence and leaves the cross-time term `−h̃(next)·h̃(now)` unsigned; her Assumption 9 is the
  money-unit inequality `R'(K)(E h̃)² ≤ 0`, while the term to be signed carries heterogeneous
  marginal-utility weights and the redistribution term; the root is not shown to be interior. The
  2023 version was not read.

## Three further approaches, 2026-11-11

**1. Uniqueness implies recursive.** Already in the repo (`Recursive.lean`,
`UniqueSequentialEquilibrium.consistentSelection`). New instance, `RepAgentUnique.lean`: with one
idiosyncratic state, sequential equilibrium from a point mass is **unique**, for every `γ > 0` and
every capital share (`KSModel.eq_of_isSequentialEquilibrium_dirac`, `Coleman.eq_capPath`). The
argument is the decentralised planner's problem: prices support the allocation
(`CobbDouglas.repCash_le_of_prices`, the tangent line, clamps included), so along one equilibrium
tree the household affords at the other's prices what it does at its own; the value gap is
superharmonic on the tree, hence nonnegative, both ways; the unique Bellman maximiser finishes.

**2. A homothetic economy with idiosyncratic risk** (`HomotheticLog.lean`). All income
proportional to wealth, an idiosyncratic income shock `θ(e)` with conditional mean one (Markov,
persistence in higher moments allowed), no insurance, log utility. Saving the fraction `β` of cash
is optimal in the overtaking sense against every positive capital path (`logPlan_isOptimal`;
transversality in closed form, `β^{N+1}/(1−β)`); the shocks average out at every aggregate node
(`condMean_logWealth`); and `K' = β g(z, K) K` is a sequential equilibrium from every initial
shock and every finitely supported wealth distribution (`isSequentialEquilibrium_logPath`),
recursive on `(z, K)` although the wealth distribution spreads out. This is a separate model, not
a `KSModel`: labour income is a share of wealth, not an additive endowment.
**CRRA** (`HomotheticCRRA.lean`, `HomotheticRate.lean`, 2026-11-11). With `γ > 0` the saving
rate is common to all households but depends on the aggregate state. It solves the rate equation
`(1 − s)^{−γ} s^γ = β Σ π m g(z', K')^{1−γ} (1 − s')^{−γ}`, `K' = s g(z, K) K`, where
`m(z, z') = E[θ^{1−γ}]` must be the same from every idiosyncratic state.
* Any `γ > 0`: a solution with rates in `(0, s̄]`, `s̄ < 1`, gives optimal plans (overtaking;
  transversality decays like `s̄^N`, `IsRateTree.ratePlan_isOptimal`) and a sequential equilibrium
  recursive on `(z, K)` (`IsRateFunction.isSequentialEquilibriumCRRA_ratePath`).
* **Every `γ > 0`**: a solution exists on an interval `[lo, hi]` (`RateData.exists_rate`,
  `HomotheticEconomy.exists_recursiveEquilibriumCRRA`). The operator is on consumption levels,
  not rates: with `y = g K`, `c = (1 − s) y`, the rate equation is the Euler equation
  `c^{−γ} = Σ μ g(z', K') c(z', K')^{−γ}`, `K' = y − c`, whose right-hand side is nonincreasing in
  `K'` for every `γ` once consumption is nondecreasing in capital. Lattice: consumption between
  the shares `1 − s̄` and `1 − s̲` of resources, consumption and saving nondecreasing; unique root,
  comparative statics, Tarski. (A first version worked in rates and needed `γ < 1`; superseded.)
  `Coleman.Data` could not be reused (it needs income bounded away from zero and `β R̄ ≤ 1`).
* Hypotheses: `g` positive, continuous, nonincreasing and `g K` nondecreasing on `[lo, hi]`, and
  rate bounds with `s̲^γ ≤ Σ μ g(z', x)^{1−γ} ≤ s̄^γ` on the interval (`low`, `high`) that keep
  capital in it (`inv_lo`, `inv_hi`). At a deterministic steady state (`lo = hi = K*`,
  `s̲ = s̄ = β m`, `g(K*) = 1/(β m)`) they hold with equality. Around it, with `a = g(lo)/g* > 1`
  and `c = g(hi)/g* < 1`: for `γ > 1` the invariance conditions are `a^{1/γ} ≥ 1`, `c^{1/γ} ≤ 1`,
  always true; for `γ < 1`, with `r = (1 − γ)/γ`, they are `c^r a ≥ 1`, `a^r c ≤ 1`, true for
  `a = 1/c` iff `γ ≥ 1/2`. The bounds are uniform, so large shocks can violate them.
*Not done*: uniqueness of the rate function.

So the proportional economy now has existence, uniqueness and a recursive `(z, K)` equilibrium at
every capital share. Away from zero exposure the minimal-state question is still open.

## Lee re-read, and log-linearity (2026-11-11)

* **Lee, *Dancing on the Saddles* (August 2026 version on disk), re-read for the step from
  spell-wise to global `(z, K)` sufficiency.** There is none, by the paper's own account. Exact
  sufficiency is within a frozen-regime spell (Theorem 1, Proposition 8, under Assumptions 1–2 and
  a no-plateau condition; Appendix E gives a route from primitives for log utility and
  Cobb–Douglas, with two conditions maintained). Across spells the paper gives a bound under an
  assumed uniform contraction (Theorem 2, Proposition 9) and calls the result near-global: the
  regime history selects which conditional saddle the economy is on. So `KSufficient` cannot be
  discharged from Lee's argument; the repo already has Theorems 1 and 2
  (`Synchronization.lean`).
* **Log-linearity fails** (`HomotheticLogLinear.lean`). In the homothetic log economy the exact
  `(z, K)` law is `K' = β((1 − δ)K + A(z)K^α)` with Cobb–Douglas, and `log K'` is affine in
  `log K` iff `δ = 1` (`cdLaw_powerLaw_iff`, `cdLaw_logLinear_iff`,
  `HomotheticEconomy.lawK_logLinear_iff`). Proof: a power law has `f(2)² = f(1)f(4)`; here the
  two sides differ by `(1 − δ)A(2^α − 2)²`.
