\section{What is open}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{$(z,K)$ at interior, persistent calibrations with price feedback.} The corner
  economy of Section~\ref{sec:corner} has persistent shocks and Cobb--Douglas prices but assets at
  corners; the economy of Section~\ref{sec:interior} has interior saving but i.i.d.\ shocks, prices
  that do not respond to capital, and an impatient household. Individual nonlinearity of saving
  does not by itself defeat $(z,K)$-sufficiency --- only the cross-sections the equilibrium visits
  matter --- so the question is open in both directions at Krusell and Smith's calibration. Price
  feedback inside the order route needs saving to rise with the capital path (which fails for
  Cobb--Douglas) and a uniqueness theorem for complementarity economies.
\item \textbf{Small forecast errors at realistic parameters.} Theorem~\ref{thm:approx} bounds the
  error of forecasting with $(z,K)$ by the nonlinearity of saving and the price feedback, over the
  whole asset range. Making it small needs the nonlinearity measured only on the cross-sections the
  equilibrium visits, where few households are near the constraint.
\item \textbf{Measurable equilibria beyond small shares.} Stationarity directly from the sequential
  equilibrium needs the sequence-state equilibrium to be measurable in the history; outside the
  small-share class the fixed point gives no such guarantee. The natural fixed-point space ---
  measurable maps into distributions, with the weak-* topology --- is compact and convex, and
  capital at each node is continuous on it; but the household's policy is nonlinear in the whole
  price path, which converges only weakly in the history, so the update is not continuous. Running
  the economy from the far past and taking limits fails for the same reason. A contraction (as for
  small shares) or randomisation (as in \citet{dgmm1994}) removes the obstruction; a pure measurable
  equilibrium without either needs a quantitative modulus of the equilibrium price path in the
  history.
\item \textbf{The minimal state without uniqueness, for Cobb--Douglas.} The greatest-equilibrium
  route needs saving to rise with the capital path. With Cobb--Douglas prices higher capital raises
  the cash of the asset-poor and lowers that of the asset-rich (the sign of $Ke-La$), so an order
  ranking households by that sign, or by cash on hand, would be needed; whether Tarski's argument
  survives with such an order is the first open step. This is the question \citet{cao2020} leaves
  open.
\item \textbf{Uniqueness at realistic capital shares.} Theorem~\ref{thm:smallshare} is qualitative;
  sharper bounds on the consumption floor and on curvature would be needed for a quantitative
  threshold.
\item \textbf{Full spotlessness}, open also in \citet{cao2020}; and the asset cap outside the
  small-share class (Theorem~\ref{thm:cap} needs the minimal-MPC slope below one).
\end{enumerate}
