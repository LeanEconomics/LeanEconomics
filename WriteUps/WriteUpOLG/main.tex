\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This is the record of a programme to formalise overlapping-generations economies in Lean 4, and of
one result it reaches. In Diamond's two-period economy the steady state is unique for every relative
risk aversion and every depreciation rate, with no condition on the savings function, even though the
saving rate itself falls with the return whenever risk aversion exceeds one: the wage channel of a
constant-returns technology dominates. That is worth stating first because it shows a uniqueness proof
need not pass through the household-side comparative static at all. A household living $J$ periods
against uninsurable earnings risk and a borrowing constraint solves a finite backward induction, so
everything proved for the infinite-horizon Bellman operator with an arbitrary continuation applies
stage by stage with no fixed point; on top of that we prove the finite-horizon sandwich
$\kappa_km\le c_k\le\kappa_k(m+H_k(z))$, that $\kappa_k$ is exactly the reciprocal of
$\sum_{i\le k}(\Thorn/R)^i$, and that the propensity to consume is at least $\kappa_k$ --- the
companion of the upper bound on its slope, and the piece the uniqueness argument turns out to need.
Adding retirement and a pension settles a claim often made informally: a means test does make the
policy non-monotone, but not for the reason usually given, since saving rises with cash on hand
whatever the continuation, by Topkis, so a non-concave value function cannot by itself reverse it.
A stationary equilibrium exists with both boundary conditions discharged from primitives: capital
supply is a finite composition, needing no stationary distribution, no Feller property and no Doeblin
condition, and it is bracketed by affine bounds on a cohort's accumulated assets whose coefficients
are explicit recursions in the primitives.

The equilibrium is unique at logarithmic utility. Single crossing does not need capital supply to rise
with the rate, only to fall more slowly than the firm's demand, and that increment is controlled by an
envelope whose per-stage multiplier is damped by the propensity floor; the mass weighting in it is
exact, because a cohort's earnings marginal is the invariant law at every age; and the multiplier
itself is one plus the variance of the risk tilt, which we certify below what the damping allows, in
exact rational arithmetic, at every one of the sixty stages and over the whole asset range. Theorem 1
then holds at every state and every age, capital supply rises with the rate, and the equilibrium is
unique --- a theorem with one computational hypothesis discharged by certificate rather than by proof
term, at one calibration.

Above logarithmic utility it is open, and the paper is as much a record of where it is blocked. Two
routes are closed with quantified reasons. Date-by-date Lasry--Lions monotonicity fails in this
economy at every risk aversion including logarithmic, and not because the price channel runs
backwards --- it is exactly the congestion term --- but because strict concavity reweights it; the
summed condition that the argument actually uses does hold, but its weakest form collapses to
monotone capital supply, so the mean field route is not weaker than the single crossing it was meant
to bypass. And the certainty-equivalent criterion that carries the logarithmic case fails from
$\gamma\approx1.5$: the propagation is damped at every risk aversion we test, and it is the
certainty-equivalent side that gives way.
\end{abstract}
